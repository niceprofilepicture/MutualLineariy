\author{Timur Aslyamov}

\date{\today}


\begin{abstract}
We show that mutual linearity of stationary probabilities and currents in Markov jump
processes follows directly from classical response theory.
Using the exact finite-response identity expressed through the Drazin
inverse, we show that changing the rates of one edge changes all
stationary probabilities by the same scalar factor multiplied by fixed
coefficients. Eliminating this factor gives the known mutual-linearity
relations, valid arbitrarily far from equilibrium. We generalize this property beyond Markov jump process to linear systems. 
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Stationary probabilities and currents generally depend nonlinearly on transition rates. Nevertheless, varying the rates of one edge produces affine relations between these quantities, known as mutual linearity~\cite{Harunari2024,Khodabandehlou2025,DalCengio2025,BebonSpeck2026,ZhengLu2026,VoitsSchwarz2026}. We aim to give a short derivation of mutual linearity from the classical finite-response identity~\cite{Schweitzer1968,Meyer1980}.
Using the Drazin inverse, this identity shows that all probability
changes share one scalar factor. Eliminating it, we find the known
relations. In addition we formulate mutual linearity extension beyond Markov processes to linear systems.

\section{Exact stationary response}
\label{sec:response}

The finite-response identity shown below dates back to the
1960s~\cite{Schweitzer1968} and was originally formulated for Markov chains. Here we simply rewrite it for the continuum time Markov jump processes over the finite state space.

We consider a finite irreducible Markov jump process, $\partial_t\bm p=W\bm p$, where $W_{ij}$ is the transition rate from $j$ to $i$, and $W_{jj}=-\sum_{i\ne j}W_{ij}$.
The stationary distribution satisfies $W\bm\pi=0$ and $\bm1^\intercal\bm\pi=1$.
Introducing a second generator $W'=W+\Delta W$, the classical fundamental-matrix identity takes the form
\begin{align}
    \Delta\bm\pi\equiv\bm\pi'-\bm\pi=-G\Delta W\bm\pi'\,,
    \label{eq:finite}
\end{align}
where $G=W^D$ is the Drazin inverse~\cite{Meyer1975,crook2018drazin,aslyamov2025excess}.
It satisfies $GW=WG=I-\bm\pi\bm1^\intercal$, $G\bm\pi=0$, and $\bm1^\intercal G=0$.
Indeed, subtracting the two stationary equations gives $W\Delta\bm\pi=-\Delta W\bm\pi'$.
Multiplication by $G$ and normalization provides \cref{eq:finite}, without assuming that $\Delta W$ is small. 

\section{Mutual linearity}
\label{sec:mutual-linearity}

\subsection{State observables}

We start with derivation of mutual linearity for probabilities and state observables. 
For the second matrix $W'$, we change $W_{ij}$ and $W_{ji}$,
keeping all other non-diagonal rates fixed and adjusting the diagonal elements to preserve probability conservation.
Then, only components $i$ and $j$ of the vector $\Delta W\bm\pi'$ are nonzero, and they have opposite signs. Equation~\eqref{eq:finite} therefore gives
\begin{align}
    \Delta\pi_n
    &=(G_{nj}-G_{ni})
      (\Delta W_{ij}\pi_j'-\Delta W_{ji}\pi_i')
    \nonumber\\
    &\equiv h_n\alpha\,,
    \label{eq:edge-response}
\end{align}
where $h_n\equiv G_{nj}-G_{ni}$ is calculated from the rates before the change,
and do not depend on $\Delta W_{ij}$ and $\Delta W_{ji}$.
All dependence on these rate changes enters the second factor
$\alpha \equiv \Delta W_{ij}\pi_j'-\Delta W_{ji}\pi_i'$.
Repeating \cref{eq:edge-response} for state $m$ and eliminating $\alpha$ gives
\begin{align}
    \Delta\pi_n=\frac{h_n}{h_m}\Delta\pi_m\,,
    \qquad h_m\ne0\,,
    \label{eq:probabilities}
\end{align}
where $(\pi_m',\pi_n')$ lies on one straight line for every choice of
the new edge rates. It was recently discovered in Ref.~\cite{BebonSpeck2026}.

More generally, for state observables $O=\sum_n O_n\pi_n$ and
$Q=\sum_n Q_n\pi_n$ with fixed weights, the same argument gives the following.
\begin{align}
    \Delta O=\frac{\sum_n O_n h_n}{\sum_n Q_n h_n}\Delta Q\,,
    \label{eq:observables}
\end{align}
provided that the denominator is nonzero.

Let us now consider \cref{eq:edge-response} for linear responses:
\begin{align}
    \partial_{W_{ij}}\pi_n=h_n\pi_j\,,\qquad
    \partial_{W_{ji}}\pi_n=-h_n\pi_i\,,
    \label{eq:derivatives}
\end{align}
so that $-\partial_{W_{ij}}\pi_n/\partial_{W_{ji}}\pi_n=\pi_j/\pi_i$
whenever $h_n\ne0$. It recovers the response ratio of
Ref.~\cite{BebonSpeck2026}.
Interestingly, that our dynamical fluctuation-response theory~\cite{AslyamovEsposito2026} gives the same ratio for
dynamical responses to impulsive rate perturbations at time $t$, with
$\pi_j/\pi_i$ replaced by $p_j(t)/p_i(t)$.
Thus, \cref{eq:derivatives} does not require mutual linearity under finite rate changes.

\subsection{Currents}

Now we turn to mutual lienarity between the steady-state currents. The stationary current from $l$ to $k$ is
$J_{kl}\equiv W_{kl}\pi_l-W_{lk}\pi_k$.
For an edge whose transition rates $W_{kl}$ and $W_{lk}$ are kept fixed,
\cref{eq:edge-response} gives
\begin{align}
    \Delta J_{kl}=(W_{kl}h_l-W_{lk}h_k)\alpha\,,
    \label{eq:output-current}
\end{align}
where changes induced by a new steady state only. 
On the perturbed edge, the rates themselves also change:
\begin{align}
    \Delta J_{ij}
    &=W_{ij}\Delta\pi_j-W_{ji}\Delta\pi_i
      +\Delta W_{ij}\pi_j'-\Delta W_{ji}\pi_i'
    \nonumber\\
    &=(1+W_{ij}h_j-W_{ji}h_i)\alpha\,.
    \label{eq:input-current}
\end{align}
Eliminating $\alpha$ between the currents on $(i,j)$ and another edge
$(k,l)$ gives
\begin{align}
    \Delta J_{kl}
    =\frac{W_{kl}h_l-W_{lk}h_k}
          {1+W_{ij}h_j-W_{ji}h_i}\Delta J_{ij}\,,
    \label{eq:currents}
\end{align}
which is the mutual linearity of stationary
currents~\cite{Harunari2024,Khodabandehlou2025,DalCengio2025}.
Both current changes contain the same $\alpha$, so their ratio is independent of the new edge rates.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:discussion}

The argument applies to any linear
system $A\bm x=\bm b$ with nonsingular $A$ and fixed $\bm b$.
If only $A_{ij}$ changes and $A'$ is nonsingular, subtracting the
equations and eliminating $\Delta A_{ij}x_j'$ between $n$ and $m$ gives
\begin{align}
    \Delta x_n&=-(A^{-1})_{ni}\Delta A_{ij}x_j'\,,\nonumber\\
    \Delta x_n&=\frac{(A^{-1})_{ni}}{(A^{-1})_{mi}}\Delta x_m\,,
    \qquad (A^{-1})_{mi}\ne0\,.
    \label{eq:linear-system}
\end{align}
The same proportionality holds for fixed linear observables.

The same argument applies whenever $\Delta A\bm x'=\bm u\alpha$,
with fixed $\bm u$: the perturbation enters the equations in fixed
proportions, with only its scalar amplitude $\alpha$ changing.
Then $\Delta\bm x=-A^{-1}\bm u\alpha$, so eliminating $\alpha$
again gives mutual linearity.

The frequency-domain extension could follow from \cref{eq:linear-system} using $A=sI-W$. For time-independent rates, the Laplace-transformed master equation is
$(sI-W)\widetilde{\bm p}(s)=\bm p(0)$.
We keep the same initial distribution in both systems and define the resolvent matrix
$R(s)=(sI-W)^{-1}$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$.
Changing the rates of edge $(i,j)$ and eliminating the common factor
between states $n$ and $m$ results in 
\begin{align}
    \Delta\widetilde p_n
    &=h_n(s)(\Delta W_{ij}\widetilde p_j'
              -\Delta W_{ji}\widetilde p_i')\,,\nonumber\\
    \Delta\widetilde p_n
    &=\frac{h_n(s)}{h_m(s)}\Delta\widetilde p_m\,,
    \qquad h_m(s)\ne0\,,
    \label{eq:laplace}
\end{align}
where $h_n(s)=R_{ni}(s)-R_{nj}(s)$ and where all probabilities are evaluated at the same $s$.
This recovers the Laplace-domain probability relations~\cite{ZhengLu2026,VoitsSchwarz2026}.
Repeating \cref{eq:output-current,eq:input-current,eq:currents} with
$\widetilde{\bm p}(s)$ and $h_n(s)$ gives the corresponding current
relations~\cite{Harunari2024}.
These relations hold for the Laplace transforms. At a fixed time, varying the edge rates need not produce a straight-line relation between two probabilities or two currents~\cite{VoitsSchwarz2026}.
